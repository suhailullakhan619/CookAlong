- 1 cup whole urad dal (black gram)
- 1/3 cup rajma (kidney beans)
- 6 cups water
- salt
- red chilli powder
- 1 tsp turmeric powder
- 1 tbsp grated ginger
- 4 tbsp oil
- 1 pinch hing (asafoetida)
- 2 bay leaves
- 1/2 cinnamon stick
- 3 laung (cloves)
- 1 onion, finely chopped
- 1 tbsp ginger garlic paste
- 3 tomatoes, finely chopped
- 2 tsp coriander powder
- 1/2 cup fresh cream
- 1 tbsp ghee
- 2 tbsp coriander leaves, chopped
> Wash the urad dal and rajma and soak them overnight in lukewarm water.
> Drain, then cook the dal and rajma in the water with salt, red chilli powder, turmeric powder and grated ginger until soft. Keep aside.

@ aam-panna | Aam Panna | Tangy summer drink concentrate of cooked green mango, roasted cumin, mint and rock salt. | 20 | 15 | 20 | indian,north-indian,drink | Cookbook:Green Mango and Cumin Drink (Aam Panna)
- 4 green mangoes, medium
- 1/2 cup sugar
- 2 tbsp rock salt
- 1 tsp roasted cumin seeds
- 1 roasted chilli
- 1 handful fresh mint leaves
> Cut each green mango into 3 pieces: one from each side of the seed and one with the seed.
> Pressure cook the mangoes with a small cup of water, turning off the heat at the first whistle.
> Let the cooker rest for 7 minutes, then open it and let the mangoes cool.
> Grind the sugar, roasted cumin seeds and roasted chilli in a food processor.
> Mince the mint leaves in the food processor.
> Squeeze the pulp of the cooled mangoes into a blender, discarding the skins and seeds.
> Purée the mango pulp until smooth.
> Add the ground sugar mixture, rock salt and mint leaves, and blend well.
> Pour into a clean bottle and refrigerate; it keeps for about 2 weeks. {notimer}
> To serve, stir 4-5 tsp of the concentrate into a glass of water.

@ baati | Baati (Rajasthani Hard Bread) | Dense, unleavened wheat discs kneaded very firm, cooked in a dry pan, charred lightly and served with ghee. | 20 | 30 | 4 | indian,north-indian,baking | Cookbook:Indian Hard Tack (Baati)
- 450 g wheat flour
- 1/2 tsp salt
- 3 tbsp oil
- water
- 1 1/2 tsp ghee
> Mix the wheat flour and salt.
> Working with about 1 cup of the flour mixture at a time, rub in a little of the oil, breaking up any lumps.
> Add water 1 tbsp at a time and knead very hard until the dough holds in a firm, compact ball. Repeat with the rest.
> Let the dough balls rest for 5 minutes.
> Flatten each ball by hand, then roll into smooth discs about 10 cm across and 1 cm thick.
> Cook in a dry pan on a low flame. Flip once the bottom starts to harden, then pinch the top all over with two fingers.
> When the bottom browns in spots, turn the baati upside-down onto a wire grate over the flame to blacken the bumps a little.
> Drizzle with the ghee and serve with dal or a vegetable dish.
