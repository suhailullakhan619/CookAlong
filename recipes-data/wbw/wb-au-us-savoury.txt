- 125 g strong bread flour

- 1 1/2 tsp bicarbonate of soda
- 8 g fresh yeast
- 75 ml lukewarm whole milk
- 1/2 tsp salt
- 250 ml lukewarm water
> Sift the bread flour and plain flour into a large bowl, adding 1/2 tsp of the bicarbonate of soda and the salt.
> Crumble the fresh yeast into a small bowl and slowly stir in the lukewarm water until you have a brown paste.

> Dissolve the rest of the bicarbonate of soda in the lukewarm milk and slowly stir it into the batter.
> Heat a lightly greased frying pan until very hot. Drop in spoonfuls of batter and cook for 2 minutes on each side, turning when the bubbles dry out.

@ sticky-date-pudding | Sticky Date Pudding | Moist, dark Australian date cake, soaked with hot brown sugar sauce and served with cream. | 30 | 90 | 12 | australian,sweet,baking | Cookbook:Sticky Date Pudding
- 2 cups brown sugar
- 1 cup water
- 1 cup pitted dates
- 1 tsp bicarbonate of soda, for the dates
- 2 cups plain flour
- 1 tsp bicarbonate of soda, for the flour
- 1/2 tsp salt
- 2 tsp mixed spice
- 4 eggs, at room temperature
- 1 1/2 cups castor sugar
- 1 cup vegetable oil
- 2 tsp vanilla essence
- 500 ml cream
> Make the sauce: stir the brown sugar and water in the top of a double boiler over simmering water until the sugar dissolves. Take off the heat and let it cool.
> Chop the dates roughly and put them in a saucepan with 1 tsp bicarbonate of soda and just enough water to cover.
> Bring to the boil and simmer for 1 minute. When it froths up to the top of the pan, take it off the heat and let it cool.
> Preheat the oven to 180 °C (350 °F). Line a large cake tin with parchment paper and grease it well.
> Sift the plain flour, 1 tsp bicarbonate of soda, salt and mixed spice together twice.
> Beat the eggs lightly, add the castor sugar and whip until very light and fluffy, as for a sponge.
> Fold in the vegetable oil and vanilla essence, without over-mixing, so you keep the air.
> Fold in the sifted flour mixture, then the cooled date mixture.
> Pour into the tin and bake for 80-90 minutes, until a skewer comes out dry. Leave the pudding in the tin. {t=5100 Bake}
> Prick the hot pudding all over with a skewer and pour about 1 cup of the sauce over it so it soaks in.
> Slice and serve at once with cream or ice cream.

- 450 g canned clams, minced
- 2 tbsp plain flour
- 1 potato, diced
- 1 onion, diced
- 1 cup boiling water
- 3 oz salt pork, diced
- 1.4 l milk
- 1 tbsp butter
- salt
- pepper
> Drain the clams and keep the liquid. Cut away the hard parts of the clams and keep them separate from the soft parts.
> Fry the salt pork in a heavy pot until brown and crisp, then lift it out.
> Fry the hard parts of the clams and the onion in the pork fat until the onion is light brown.
> Stir in the flour, then slowly add the boiling water, salt, pepper and diced potato.
> Cover and simmer gently for 10 minutes, until the potato is tender.
> Add the soft parts of the clams and the clam liquid.
> Scald the milk. Just before serving, stir in the scalded milk and the butter, and heat through without boiling.
> Serve piping hot with crackers.
